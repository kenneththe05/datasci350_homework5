Author

Kenneth He

Published

February 25, 2026

This report analyses a sample of the World Development Indicators (WDI) dataset for 2022. The data source is the World Bank WDI database (World Bank 2022).

1 Exploratory Data Analysis

We focus on three key indicators for 2022: GDP per capita, life expectancy, and inflation rate. These variables capture economic development, health outcomes, and macroeconomic stability.

The summary statistics reveal substantial cross-country variation in economic conditions. GDP per capita has a mean of about 20,346 USD but a much lower median of 7,588 USD, indicating strong right-skewness driven by high-income outliers and large dispersion.

Life expectancy is less variable, with a mean of 72.4 years and a relatively small standard deviation, suggesting more moderate differences across countries.

Inflation exhibits the greatest volatility. Although the median rate is around 8 percent, extreme maximum values (above 170 percent) inflate both the mean and standard deviation, confirming the presence of severe outliers and macroeconomic instability in some countries.

3 Summary of Key Indicators

df[["gdp_per_capita","life_expectancy","inflation_rate"]] \
  .agg(["mean","median","std"]) \
  .T \
  .round(2) \
  .set_axis(["Mean","Median","Std Dev"], axis=1)

Table 1 reports the mean, median, and standard deviation for GDP per capita, life expectancy, and inflation rate in 2022. GDP per capita exhibits substantial dispersion, reflecting large differences in income levels across countries. Life expectancy shows comparatively lower variability, while inflation displays greater volatility, consistent with the presence of extreme values observed earlier. Overall, the table reinforces the unequal distribution of economic and macroeconomic conditions worldwide.

The positive relationship between income and life expectancy is consistent with classic findings in the development literature (Preston 1975).
